# iris: the board as a technical drawing

- Concept: the board is a drawing of your work. One sheet in a hairline frame, a row of header cells, full-height lane lines, a title block (how many are open and done, how much is archived, when it was drawn, the look's code IR–1).
- Line: every rule, frame and drawing is the thinnest line the browser draws (`--hair` 1px, .5px on Retina, .34px at 3x; `--sw` for SVG strokes). Drawings and Safari's borders are one device pixel; Chrome rounds a border up to one CSS pixel. Hierarchy comes from contrast (`--rule` vs `--rule-2`), never from thicker lines.
- Type: Archivo Extra Condensed 800 for counts (all one size; Needs you in the accent), Archivo Expanded capitals for labels, Inter for everything read (17 px titles 600, 16 px text 400), codes included (figures of one width, a slashed zero). Two families, no third face for codes.
- Colour: grey paper `#e8e8e4` / night `#0b0b0a`, ink, and one accent only for what needs you (the eight presets, tuned per theme; `tokens.test.ts` keeps each readable).
- Lane head: count and the lane's object drawn with one line (bulb, tape reel that turns while something works, bell, hourglass, moon, flag), the name, one fact (`facts.ts`) and a timeline: one tick per session on a log scale, two weeks ago to now, standing on the lane rule with faint marks an hour, a day and a week ago.
- Paper: a faint dot grid under everything; the header row stands on a scale (a tick every 8 px, a longer one every 40). By day the Needs you lane is washed with the accent; at night it is not (a tint on near-black turns muddy) and every line is one warm grey, frames brighter than the lines inside, as on a printed data sheet. Menus and popovers float as a hairline sheet with a second one behind it. Projects get one muted hue each, on their tab's tick and strip only.
- Grid: Needs you as framed cards; a folded or empty lane is a drawer (object, name, fact, timeline, count).
- Motion: little, and only when it means something. The board arrives (lane rules draw themselves, counts rise, cards follow one by one); the eye follows the pointer (its pupil narrows up close), looks at Needs you when the pointer rests and raises three lashes while something waits there, and blinks now and then, sometimes twice (`eyeFollow.ts`, as ink's eye); the bell rings while something waits on you, the hourglass turns over while you wait on others, the moon's star twinkles; under the pointer the bulb lights and the flag waves. With reduced motion set in the system nothing moves on its own (the eye still turns to the pointer, at once), and every still frame is the drawing you see then (the e2e pages ask for reduced motion).
- Cards: the fixed frame of every look; no stamp per session. Marks hang from the card's top rule; a session whose agent is stopped waiting for you has its top rule in the accent and its `!` blinks; a working one has a short stroke travelling along its top rule. The open card gets four corner marks.
